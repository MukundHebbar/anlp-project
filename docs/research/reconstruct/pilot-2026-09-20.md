# RECONSTRUCT local pilot: procedure, experiments, and findings

Date: 2026-09-20

Follow-up interpretation: this was an exploratory pilot with small subsets and mostly one decoding seed. “Best tested” below means the provisional development choice, not statistically established superiority. The controlled follow-up protocol is in `docs/plans/reconstruct-followup-controlled.md`; its validity audit is in `followup-validity-audit.md`.

## Decision

RECONSTRUCT now runs end to end and the evaluator separates valid alternative questions, incorrect questions, superficial clue attachment, and incomplete verification. The best tested procedure is:

1. Generate one to four honest partial-solution clues with `convert_p2`.
2. Retain only banks accepted by `audit_p2`; never pad a bank.
3. Show the final answer and complete accepted bank as ordinary sentence bullets in seeded random order.
4. Ask local Qwen with compact one-example prompt `student_p1` for a self-contained question, concise grounding justification, and explicit assumptions.
5. Score source-blind with `evaluate_p2`; measure exact-source recovery separately with `source_recovery_p1`.

This is a **development candidate**, not a confirmation-frozen benchmark. It reached 3/8 grounded successes on the D1 four-clue common subset, but only 1/12 on D2. The 24-source C split was not opened. A stable medium Qwen success rate was therefore not established.

## Construct and score

RECONSTRUCT measures abductive mathematical construction under incomplete evidence. Given a final answer and partial observations from an unknown solution, the model proposes a coherent question whose answer is supplied and explains how every observation participates.

The primary object is the reconstructed question. Requiring a complete proof would partly duplicate SOLVE. The final rubric distinguishes:

- **4:** valid reconstruction with sufficient verification;
- **3:** valid, answer-correct, clue-grounded reconstruction with a remaining proof or calculation step omitted;
- **2:** plausible construction with an unsupported essential connection or substantive grounding gap;
- **1:** wrong answer, ill-posed question, contradiction, or violated clue;
- **0:** tautology, answer copying, or unrelated output.

Grounded reconstruction success is score 3 or 4 with a well-posed question, independently correct answer, no shortcut, and every clue compatible and substantively grounded. Score-4 rate is the stricter full-verification outcome. Source recovery is diagnostic: 2 is the same essential source problem, 1 is related structure with a changed condition, parameter, or target, and 0 is a different problem.

More clues improved average recovery, while mathematical success was non-monotonic. Recovery, reconstruction validity, and proof completion must remain separate outcomes.

## Format and disclosure

The matched nine-source, three-clue format study held clue identities and order fixed and used the original strict judge.

| Format | Judged | Strict successes | Mean / 4 | Exact recovery | Mean recovery / 2 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Sentence bullets | 8 | 0 | 1.25 | 1/9 | 0.67 |
| Fragments | 9 | 2 | 1.33 | 0/9 | 0.67 |
| Paragraph | 8 | 1 | 1.75 | 1/9 | 0.89 |

No format produced stable exact recovery. Sentence bullets remain default for readability and because paragraphs can imply connective structure.

On three D1 items whose low/medium/high rewrites passed audit, grounded success was 0/3 at every level. Mean recovery rose from 0.00 at low to 0.33 at medium and 1.00 at high, without exact recovery. On D2, six high-disclosure four-clue banks passed audit; all six outputs scored 1. More specificity increased semantic anchoring in the small D1 probe but did not improve answer-consistent reconstruction.

## Prompt and selection experiments

- In the initial three-source screen, zero-shot and three few-shot variants each produced 1/3 strict successes. Longer prompting showed no advantage.
- `student_p4` added a yes/no example and explicit answer-type rule. At D1 k=3 it tied the compact prompt at 1/9 grounded successes. On D2 k=4 both prompts produced 1/8, while p4 had lower mean score (1.25 versus 1.38). It was rejected.
- Always including the first contextual clue produced 1/8 judged successes, mean 1.38, and 0/9 exact recoveries. It was rejected.
- Structured orientation converter `convert_p3` passed audit on only 1/4 trials. The others restated the target or completed the proof jointly. The accepted case improved recovery from different to related but remained score 2. It was rejected.

These failures favor a simple converter plus independent audit over hand-designed clue roles.

## Controls and evaluator

In a three-source smoke test, answer-only produced 1/3 valid alternative questions but 0/3 exact recoveries. Full worked solutions produced 3/3 strict successes and 3/3 exact recoveries. Answer-compatible invention can occur without evidence, so validity must be accompanied by clue grounding and recovery diagnostics.

Final `evaluate_p2` scored all 11 authored fixtures within their accepted ranges. Fixtures cover exact and alternative valid questions, valid reconstruction with incomplete verification, an ungrounded recurrence assertion, wrong answers, violated and decorative clues, tautology, ill-posedness, and unrelated output. This is model-based calibration, not human inter-rater validation.

## D2 check and stopping decision

The final complete-bank D2 condition used all accepted clues: eight sources had four and four had three.

| Result | Value |
|---|---:|
| Grounded reconstruction success | 1/12 (8.3%) |
| Scores 0 / 1 / 2 / 3 / 4 | 0 / 10 / 1 / 0 / 1 |
| Mean score | 1.33 / 4 |
| Source recovery 0 / 1 / 2 | 2 / 9 / 1 |
| Exact source recovery | 1/12 (8.3%) |

Manual review found Qwen repeatedly ignored the supplied answer or dropped conditions even with the full bank: it asked for a derivative when the answer was a pole order, derived a fixed point inconsistent with the supplied answer, omitted coprimality from a count, and changed a limit into an unevaluated integral. Stronger clues and extra prompt instructions did not repair these failures.

Because D2 failed the proposed 35–70% useful range, running C would consume the held-out set after an unsuccessful development freeze. C remains untouched for a later task revision or stronger student.

## Conclusions

The experiments establish that:

- More clues increase semantic constraint but do not guarantee mathematical correctness.
- Exact source recovery and valid alternative construction are distinct.
- Requiring complete proof execution confounds RECONSTRUCT with SOLVE.
- Answer-only inputs can yield valid but ungrounded inventions.
- Stronger clues, longer prompts, fragments, paragraphs, and forced orientation did not reliably improve Qwen3-4B.

The task is ready for continued development and comparison with stronger students. It is not ready for a claimed medium-difficulty Qwen benchmark or held-out training-transfer experiment.

## Reproduction

```bash
scripts/run_local_qwen.sh foreground

.venv/bin/python scripts/run_reconstruct.py student \
  --output artifacts/reconstruct/pilot-v1/run.json --split D2 \
  --bank-prompt convert_p2 --student-prompt student_p1 \
  --condition bank --all-clues --style sentence --disclosure medium \
  --selection-policy random --seed 0 --clue-seed 20260920 --max-tokens 8192

.venv/bin/python scripts/run_reconstruct.py evaluate \
  --output artifacts/reconstruct/pilot-v1/run.json --split D2 \
  --response-prompt student_p1 --response-bank-prompt convert_p2 \
  --response-style sentence --response-disclosure medium \
  --response-selection-policy random --evaluate-prompt evaluate_p2 \
  --reasoning-effort low --teacher-max-tokens 8192
```

Use a new output file for a clean future run and freeze exact source IDs and bank sizes before student generation.
